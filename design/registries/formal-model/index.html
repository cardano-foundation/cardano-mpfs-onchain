    Formal model and audits

    As someone who will implement or rely on this design, I want to know exactly which guarantees are proved, how the proofs were checked, and what is not covered, so that I do not mistake a modelled property for an implemented one.

    The design is modelled in Lean 4 under lean/MpfsCage/RequestApprovalFold/. The proofs establish properties of the model. They do not establish that the validators implement it: no validator implements it yet, and conformance is a separate milestone ticket (#114).

    The model

    Part What it captures
    Trie, writes, conditions The tree as a map from keys to entries; set and unset; absent, present and equals, read on the logical tree, the tree with every witness removed; ordered application within a fold
    Durable witness A witnessed entry holding the attested value or absence, the deposit and the refund address, with one witness token in its own output at the registry state's address, bound to the request that issued it
    Registry state Registry identity, oracle identities, protocol hash, optional rule, tree, one fee balance per key, and a value equal to the sum of the balances
    Request output Registry, owner, seed, a fresh output reference, one write, conditions, a fee per oracle, deadline, window, evidence, escrow, its request token and one tag per oracle
    Judgment Registry, request, oracle identity, verdict, the record of the members that signed and, for an approval, the oracle's own conditions, all bound in its name
    Claim A claim token named after the registry, the claiming key, the destination address and datum of the payout, a deadline and a tip
    Price notice An oracle identity's price token and its current fee, optionally per protocol
    Extraction A request that names a key and the value or absence it wants witnessed, with its deposit, refund address, remainder, deadline and window, and no oracle fees
    Transactions Creation, judgment, fold, owner withdrawal, claim creation, claim withdrawal, extraction creation, extraction withdrawal, judgment burn, and price notice minting and update, each with its guard
    Fold items A write request, a complete account, a claim, an extraction and the complete account of an extraction, each applied to the state the previous item left, under one validity interval that fits every item
    Step The authoritative transition relation over a global state, labelled by the exact transaction performed

    Modelling choices the decisions left open, ruled before the model was written: a registry has at least one oracle or a rule, and no repeated oracle; the rule is an abstract predicate over one request and the creating transaction's signatures; each oracle has its own non-negative fee; creation requires escrow covering the sum of the fees plus the ledger minimum, a fixed positive parameter of the registry; time is a single observed value whose soundness against transaction validity intervals is assumed; names are collision-free structured values, standing for the hashes the implementation will use.

    Readings of the money decisions, ruled before the fee balances were stated:

    • conservation counts the registry's value on both sides: its value before, plus the request escrows and claim escrows, equals its value after, plus the request remainders, claim payouts and claim tips; the registry's permanent minimum reserve is outside this account;
    • a claim output holds its tip, which goes to the folder when the claim is folded and back to the claiming key on withdrawal; a claim folds strictly before its deadline and is withdrawn from its deadline on, so there is no gap;
    • a complete account pays the same total per oracle, and the same remainder to the owner, whichever verdicts are shown; when a group judged more than once with different signers, the folder's choice of judgment decides which of its keys are credited.

    Readings of the durable witness decisions, ruled before the witnesses were stated:

    • a witness carries a declared deposit of at least the ledger minimum, which funds its output and is refunded exactly;
    • an extraction is an ordinary request: it pays the folder its remainder and covers the ledger minimum at creation, declares no oracle fees, passes no rule, and keeps the three windows of every request; a request that never mints its witness returns the deposit to its owner;
    • a witness is burned only by a fold item that changes its key's value, and then its deposit goes to its refund address; a write of the value already held keeps it, and a witnessing set of the value already witnessed is not folded;
    • the issuer has no exit: no signature, deadline or withdrawal releases a live witness or its deposit;
    • items are applied in order, so a fold that changes a key and changes it back burns the witness at the first change; a witness minted and burned inside one fold nets out on the transaction, and its issuer is still refunded;
    • a witness of presence is a witness of an exact value read as presence: an extraction names the value it wants witnessed, and any value change burns it.

    How it was checked

    Two independent audits ran on the first candidate, and fresh ones on the repair. Each auditor worked on a frozen copy with a sealed list of inputs and never contacted the author.

    The extension for registry rules, request tokens, registries without oracles, the present condition and oracle conditions needed one repair. The statement audit found the dead-judgment burn theorem limited to approvals without conditions, and the rule-burn theorem true by definition. The mutation audit found that a rule fed a different request or different signatures, or a request token minted by the release policy despite a rule, broke no theorem. The repair restated the first two theorems over the actual transitions and added theorems binding the rule to the created request and the creating signatures, and the request token to the rule's policy. On the repair, the statement and proof audit passed every row except the uncommissioned blind reading, and the mutation audit ran 145 single-edit mutants, none of which passes the full build; 105 of 113 theorems are each killed by a relevant mutant.

    What the mutation audit could not count as killed, none of it a defect:

    • the theorem that the windows cover all time is arithmetic over time alone, so no change to the model can falsify it; the timing of each exit is protected separately;
    • a mutant that forbids an oracle from answering both ways does falsify the equivocation theorem, as a separate proof shows, but the build fails first in the examples, so the strict rule of the audit leaves it uncounted.
    • the theorems that tags are unique per request and that discovery works across registries are each guarded by an earlier theorem that the relevant mutant breaks first (registry validity, and discovery itself), so the fault is caught but not credited to them;
    • in the same way, discovery of requests across registries, the burn of the request token on every removal, the need for a rule in a registry without oracles, and the uselessness of a stale approval are each guarded by an earlier theorem (request-token discovery, the burn of tags on every exit, the impossibility of a registry with neither oracles nor rule, and the fold proving every condition) that the relevant mutant breaks first.

    Fee balances, complete accounts and claims

    flowchart TB
  c1["138 statements"] --> a1["Statement audit:<br/>claim history unbound,<br/>approval lookup weakened"]
  a1 --> c2["Repair: 141 statements"]
  c2 --> a2["Statement audit:<br/>equivocation weakened"]
  a2 --> c3["Repair: 142 statements"]
  c3 --> a3["Statement audit: passed,<br/>every row killed"]
  a3 --> p["Proofs: 142 proved"]
  p --> a4["Proof audit: passed"]

    Wiring oracle identities, fee balances, complete accounts and claims into the registry model restated 90 of the 113 earlier theorems and added 29. Every audit read the statements blind before comparing them with the decisions, and compared each restated theorem with its earlier form, so that no guarantee was weakened under a kept name.

    The first statement audit found that the theorem on claim signatures did not tie a live claim to its own history: a model that created claims unsigned, or changed a claim's payout after creation, satisfied every statement. It also found the approval lookup reduced to a list membership true by definition, and fifteen earlier theorems weaker than before. The repair bound every live claim to a signed creation on the state's own history and restored the rest. The second audit found the equivocation theorem no longer tied to a live request of the state's registry, and no statement that the exit windows do not overlap. After that repair the third audit found every statement exact, each of the 142 falsified by at least one single-edit mutant, and 155 of 157 listed guards and effects killed. The other two are equivalent mutants: an interval check that the fold already makes.

    All 142 theorems were then proved without changing any statement. An independent proof audit confirmed the elaborated types identical to the audited statements, no escape hatch, only the standard axioms, and read the money proofs against reachable examples.

    Durable witnesses

    flowchart TB
  c1["193 statements"] --> a1["Statement audit:<br/>false helper,<br/>extractions could repeat"]
  a1 --> c2["Repair: 198 statements"]
  c2 --> a2["Statement audit:<br/>witness event order free"]
  a2 --> c3["Repair: 199 statements"]
  c3 --> a3["Statement audit: passed,<br/>every row killed"]
  a3 --> p["Proofs: 199 proved"]

    Adding durable witnesses kept all 142 earlier theorems, restated 21 of them over the new transitions and added 57. The first statement audit found a supporting lemma on fold payments false once an extraction met a witnessed key, and no statement that a consumed extraction leaves for good: a model that let its owner withdraw the same escrow forever satisfied every theorem. The repair stated extraction terminality as for ordinary requests. The second audit found that a model recording a fold's witness events in reverse order, so that a witness could be consumed before the item that minted it, also satisfied every theorem; the repair stated that witness events follow item order. The third audit read every statement blind, killed a single-edit mutant for each of the 199 theorems, including a burn on a same-value write, a burn on a write to another key, an issuer-signed burn, a refund to the wrong address and an extraction without its tip, and replayed all 392 mutants in clean trees. One guard, an interval check the fold already makes, is an equivalent mutant.

    All 199 theorems were then proved without changing any statement. An independent proof audit confirmed the elaborated types and their dependencies identical to the audited statements, replayed every proof in a fresh kernel check, found no escape hatch and only the standard axioms, and read the witness and money proofs against reachable examples.

    Oracle identities

    The oracle identity standard has its own model, under lean/MpfsCage/OracleIdentity/, independent of any registry. It follows specs/oracle-identity/decisions.md; its theorem and mutant ledger is specs/oracle-identity/ledgers.md. All 31 theorems are proved with only the standard axioms.

    Guarantee Proved by
    An identity names exactly one description, and authorization binds the description supplied to the identity expected identity_injective, authorization_binds_identity, authorization_inversion
    A group is valid exactly when its threshold is between one and its number of members, every share is positive, its members are valid and no key appears twice anywhere in it; invalid descriptions authorize nothing and are paid nothing group_valid_iff, repeated_key_invalid, invalid_description_refused, split_refusal_iff
    A key authorizes exactly when it signs; a group exactly when its threshold of members authorize; adding signers never revokes authorization, and only the set of signers matters key_authorizes_iff, group_authorizes_iff, below_threshold_refused, authorization_monotone, signer_set_extensional
    A threshold of m needs m distinct signing keys, because a key belongs to at most one member of a group key_contributes_to_at_most_one_member, threshold_needs_distinct_signing_keys
    The record of signers is canonical: every authorizing member, in order, with its share, down to keys; it names only keys that signed, each once group_record_exact, key_record_exact, recorded_keys_are_signers, record_keys_unique, recorded_keys_exact, successful_record_keys_exact, signing_keys_exact
    The split pays exactly the fee: each member its floor share, the remainder to the first signed member, recursively; a key oracle receives the whole fee, as a one-member group would split_conserves_fee, group_split_exact, key_receives_whole_fee, key_is_single_member_case, recorded_split_replay
    Nobody is paid for work it did not do: a key that did not sign, and every key of a member or subgroup below its threshold at any depth, receives nothing unsigned_key_receives_nothing, nonauthorizing_member_receives_nothing, nonauthorizing_subgroup_at_any_depth_receives_nothing
    What a key receives is exactly the sum of its payments, and the amounts received sum to the total paid paid_to_exact, paid_to_partition

    Every audit ran in a fresh context on a frozen commit, with no contact with the author. The first statement audit found that no statement fixed the record of a single key: a model recording each key as an invented one-member group satisfied all 24 statements. The repair fixed the key record exactly and extended the unpaid-subgroup guarantee to any depth. The proof audit then found three changes it could not catch, all in the functions the theorems use to read payments and signers: the payment to a key forced to zero, the keys of a key record emptied, and the signer filter removed. Five more theorems pin those functions exactly, and the final audit killed all 53 mutants of its operator set, those three included.

    What this model does not cover: the hash and encoding of a description, and the cost of authorization and splitting. The registry model binds the record of signers to each judgment and credits fees by it.
